1. RAW — A Browser Rebuilt From Scratch

Take a deep breath—you are in a tracker-free zone now. RAW began from a very simple, relatable frustration: almost every mainstream browser on the market quietly treats you like the product on the shelf. Before a homepage has even finished loading, your typing rhythm, your GPU model, and even the ambient noise floor of your microphone get fingerprinted, packaged up, and handed over to an ad network (who probably knows your snack preferences better than you do). RAW is my personal answer to all that chaos—a calming, user-first browser built on Electron, but completely re-engineered underneath with its own custom window management, an uncompromising privacy shield, an intelligent AI agent, and a friendly companion hanging out right on top. (Yes, there are real notes in my project repo titled I broke-internet.md and I_was_about_to_go_mental_hospital.md from the debugging days, so I suffered so you don't have to.)

When you first open it, RAW feels like coming home to a browser you already know and love—your tabs sit neatly up top, right alongside a clean address bar and the classic back, forward, and reload trio. The real magic reveals itself the moment you start playing around with it: you can effortlessly tear tabs off into brand-new windows, drag and reorder them with buttery-smooth animations, mute noisy tabs individually, duplicate them in a heartbeat, and even bring back twenty accidentally closed tabs in the exact order you lost them. She lip-syncs to voice responses, dances along to whatever music you're playing, and gestures expressively while the AI agent is busy working on a task. Far from being a sticker slapped on top for show, she is wired directly into the exact same tool-calling pipeline that the AI agent uses to pilot the browser itself.

Grab a snack and scroll at your own pace: the rest of this page walks through every single system that makes RAW tick—from the navigation layer, anti-fingerprinting engine, and page-friendly ad blocker to native DRM streaming, the built-in media downloader, the full-system VPN, on-device speech and translation, the 33-tool AI agent, Tiyara herself, and the handy utility suite tucked underneath. 2. Tabs, Windows & Navigation

AB
Arnab Biswas
Solo Developer • Systems & Privacy Architecture
Oct 20, 2026 • 8 min read

Tab management sounds like the most relaxing weekend project in the world—right up until you actually try building it from scratch. While Chrome has had well over a decade to polish its tabs, RAW's tab architecture had to be crafted from the ground up on top of Electron's WebContentsView, and it needed to go beyond standard expectations with full cross-window tab migration, per-tab audio isolation, and a closed-tab memory stack that actually respects chronology.

  • Mindful state tracking — Creating, closing, and hopping between tabs is orchestrated by a dedicated window manager that quietly watches every tab's URL, title, favicon, load progress, back/forward history, and whether it is currently making a racket.
  • Forgiving tab recovery — Accidentally closing a tab during a late-night research spiral isn't a tragedy here; the last 20 closed tabs are safely held on a per-window stack so hitting "reopen closed tab" brings them back in the exact order they left.
  • Seamless window hopping — You can pluck a tab right out of one window and drop it cozily into another, which under the hood means detaching and re-adopting a live WebContentsView mid-session without causing a single hiccup in whatever video is playing inside it.
  • Popup peace of mind — Annoying popups don't get to litter your screen with chaotic new windows; a smart window-open handler catches them at the door and tucks them neatly into contained tabs instead.
  • Secure internal routing & domain locks — Beyond everyday web surfing, RAW uses a custom internal app:// protocol to serve its own UI pages (like settings, tools, and history) in complete isolation from the open web, paired with an enterprise-grade domain lock that can strictly limit browsing to approved origins whenever you need guardrails.

Even printing and saving PDFs got the same loving attention to detail: a live print preview modal renders your page on the spot, crafts a crisp, lossless PDF using Chromium's native engine, or sends it straight to a physical printer without ever making you leave your cosy browser window.

3. Inside the Privacy & Anti-Fingerprinting Engine

AB
Arnab Biswas
Solo Developer • Systems & Privacy Architecture
Oct 20, 2026 • 8 min read

If RAW has a protective older sibling energy, this engine is where it lives. Most "private" browsers block third-party cookies, pat themselves on the back, and call it a day—even though cookies are the least clever way trackers follow you around anymore. The sneakiest tracking happens by inspecting your actual hardware: how your specific GPU draws a hidden canvas, the microscopic quirks of your audio stack, and how many CPU threads your machine brags about having. RAW steps right in front of all of that so you can browse in peace.

  • Fail-closed partitioning — RAW flatly refuses to let any traffic wander through Electron's default session partition, routing everything instead through two strictly isolated, purpose-built partitions so accidental default-session leaks are impossible.
  • Canvas noise injection — Every time a script calls getImageData(), RAW stirs in a tiny, invisible pinch of mathematical noise so your canvas fingerprint looks different every session while keeping the visual page looking completely normal to your eyes.
  • WebGL disguise kit — Your GPU vendor strings, renderer names, and shader parameters are dynamically masked, preventing nosy scripts from recognizing your computer by its graphics card.
  • Audio fingerprint masking — Output from AudioContext receives the same imperceptible mathematical seasoning, shutting the door on acoustic device profiling without changing how your music sounds.
  • Hardware concurrency spoofing — I made RAW politely fib about navigator.hardwareConcurrency so trackers can't use your CPU thread count to pick you out of a crowd.
  • Session-seeded consistency — All of this protective noise is generated from a seed that stays steady for your current session (so web apps don't get confused and break mid-click) but refreshes between sessions so nobody can link today's visit to tomorrow's.

To make sure no sneaky script slips through a side door, RAW automatically attaches the Chrome DevTools Protocol debugger to every single tab, subframe, and background worker—meaning even nested iframes that think nobody is watching get the exact same privacy rules applied. Plus, there is a completely separate sandbox partition set aside just for security testing, ensuring your experimental tinkering never touches your personal browsing life.

4. Ad-Blocking That Doesn't Break Pages

Modern ad blocking is basically a never-ending game of cat and mouse: the moment blockers get good, websites start setting up "honeypot" traps just to catch you and throw up a giant guilt-trip banner asking you to turn your blocker off. RAW's ad-blocking layer was designed to outsmart the traps quietly so you can just read your article in peace.

  • Instant per-domain whitelisting — When you want to support a creator or site you genuinely trust, you can exempt their domain with a click and have it sync instantly across every rendering surface with zero awkward delay.
  • Polite stealth probe evasion — Instead of slamming the door in the face of known anti-adblock honeypot domains (which tips off the website), RAW intercepts them and hands back a harmless, invisible transparent pixel via its internal protocol—leaving the trap completely satisfied while your page stays unbroken and ad-free.
  • FadBlock for video sanity — For those stubborn YouTube ads that try to sneak past traditional filters, FadBlock steps in to fast-forward the ad playback and click the skip button the millisecond it materializes, saving you from hearing about another productivity app you'll never download.

5. Native DRM & Streaming Acceleration

AB
Arnab Biswas
Solo Developer • Systems & Privacy Architecture
Oct 20, 2026 • 8 min read

Here is a dirty little secret of indie browser development: out-of-the-box Electron Chromium builds don't have the licensing handshakes to speak Widevine or FairPlay like mainstream browsers do. Usually, that means the moment you try to unwind with Netflix, Spotify, Hulu, or Prime Video, the site either refuses to play altogether or passive-aggressively serves you a blurry potato-quality stream from 2006.

RAW fixes this headache with a bit of clever engineering magic: the moment it detects a protected streaming service, a lightweight native addon seamlessly mounts a real macOS WebKit view right on top of the Electron window for that specific tab. Because that layer is running genuine Safari-grade DRM under the hood, you get full hardware-accelerated Widevine and FairPlay decryption effortlessly. Even better, session cookies sync both ways between Electron's store and the native cookie store, and the overlay follows every window resize, move, and tab switch so smoothly that you'll never even notice a second rendering engine stepped in to save your movie night.

6. XDown — The Built-In Media Downloader

AB

You've probably been there—trying to save a video for an offline flight and ending up on a sketchy "Free Video Downloader" website covered inseventeen fake green download buttons.   • Clean in-memory caching — Streams flow through a speedy in-memory LRU cache rather than cluttering your hard drive with half-baked temporary scratch files.
  • Built-in clip studio — Want just a 30-second snippet or only the audio track? A friendly clip extractor UI lets you trim exact timestamps, choose your resolution, or pull pure audio before the file even lands in your downloads folder.

7. The Full-System VPN & Privacy Network

AB
Arnab Biswas
Solo Developer • Systems & Privacy Architecture
Oct 20, 2026 • 8 min read

When other browsers brag about a "built-in VPN," they usually mean a glorified browser proxy that leaves the rest of your computer out in the cold. RAW goes all the way with a true VPN layer powered by the VLESS + REALITY protocol over Xray Core, which cleverly disguises your VPN handshake as everyday TLS web traffic so deep packet inspection can't tell the difference. Everything runs locally on your machine and can protect either just your browser or your entire operating system at the flip of a switch.

  • Batteries included — Native multi-architecture binaries for both the proxy core and tunneling layer come bundled right inside the app, complete with full routing tables and geo-data sets.
  • Share the umbrella with mobile tethering — Any phone on your Wi-Fi can hop into your desktop's encrypted VPN tunnel just by scanning a QR code—zero tedious typing of server keys required.
  • No-nonsense kill switches — While the full-system VPN is on duty, IPv6 is automatically switched off system-wide and WebRTC is strictly locked down so your real local IP never slips out by accident.
  • Server-side ad blackholing — Known ad and tracker domains get dropped into a black hole right at the VPN layer, stopping junk packets before they even knock on your device's door.
  • Graceful network recovery — A multi-tier network interface detector makes sure that when you disconnect, your system's proxy settings are restored cleanly every single time—never leaving your Wi-Fi confused and pointing at a closed tunnel.

9. The RAW-AI Agent & Its 33 Tools

AB
Arnab Biswas
Solo Developer • Systems & Privacy Architecture

Here is where RAW transforms from a cosy, well-armoured browser into an active co-pilot that can actually take the wheel when you're tired of clicking. Mapped out in detail across my architecture blueprints (RAW_AI_Architecture.svg, RAW_AI_Logic_Flow.mmd, and RAW_AI_Tools_Map.svg), the AI orchestrator smoothly routes your requests between local Ollama models and cloud models—automatically falling back without a fuss if a cloud quota gets full, and streaming answers via ultra-fast NDJSON straight from local models whenever that's the quicker route.

Instead of just chatting from the sidelines, the agent has 33 hands-on tools organized into five practical toolkits:

  • Full browser automation — It can list, open, close, switch, navigate, duplicate, or restore tabs; click buttons and fill out forms by accessibility ID; scroll, search inside pages, pop open DevTools, or execute raw JavaScript directly inside the page context while you sip your tea.
  • Visual & multimodal perception — It can snap gated screenshots, render a whole page into a PDF stamped with numbered badges over every clickable element for pinpoint visual targeting, or run a reverse image search straight from a local file.
  • On-demand translation — Ask it to translate a highlighted paragraph or an entire foreign webpage and it handles it on the spot.
  • Interactive canvas & code validation — It can summon pre-built UI widgets (clocks, live weather cards, study flashcards, interactive quizzes), load structured skill runbooks, and pre-validate math, 3D graphs, molecular structures, or Desmos-style code against dedicated rendering engines before showing it to you so you never get a broken chart.
  • Audio, weather & 3D companion controls — It can flip your EQ audio presets, check the local forecast, or choreograph Tiyara's animations and poses right from the middle of your conversation.

For complete peace of mind, every multi-turn session is recorded with tamper-proof HMAC-SHA256 signed audit logs that track token usage and cost per turn—and a multi-tier abort button lets you stop the agent (and any background sub-agents) mid-thought if you ever change your mind.
